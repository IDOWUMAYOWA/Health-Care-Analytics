Read this before using the numbers

Several fields in the source data are internally inconsistent. The dashboard reports what the data says; this page records where it should not be trusted. Every figure below is reproducible from the source CSVs.

Findings

Ordered by impact on the reported figures

Clinical categories are not linked to each otherBlocks analysis

824 outpatient visits carry a room type, and 844 of the 1,245 inpatient visits have no admission date. 760 visits with service type "Emergency" are flagged as not being an emergency visit. Service type, room type, admission dates and the emergency flag appear to have been populated independently, so any analysis that combines them — occupancy, true inpatient cost, emergency throughput — cannot be trusted.

One month missing, two distortedDistorts trend

November 2024 contains no visits at all. October 2024 has 45 against a monthly norm of roughly 300, and January 2025 has 925. The most likely explanation is that records were shifted rather than lost, which would make the January spike the missing October and November volume. Until confirmed, month-over-month comparisons across that window are meaningless.

Satisfaction scores are bimodalInterpret with care

52.6% of visits score 1 or 2, and the remaining scores are almost uniformly distributed from 3 to 10. This is not the shape a real satisfaction survey produces. The mean of 3.84 is arithmetically correct but describes a distribution that has no clinical meaning, so provider comparisons on satisfaction are directional at best.

Insurance coverage is missing on 119 visitsMinor

Treated as zero coverage in these calculations, which slightly overstates patient liability. A further 2 visits have coverage exceeding the gross bill; these are clipped at zero rather than allowed to become negative revenue.

Room charges depend on a derived length of stayMethod note

The source provides a daily room rate but no room total. Length of stay is calculated as discharge date minus admission date, and room revenue as rate multiplied by that duration. Visits with no admission date contribute no room revenue. Given the inconsistency noted above, this understates room income if admissions are genuinely missing rather than genuinely absent.
